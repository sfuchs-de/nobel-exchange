# Nobel Exchange candidate review

Reviewed October 8, 2026. Scope: all 90 economists in the local game roster. This is a review and a proposed editorial treatment, not a change to the game.

## Overall assessment

Most names belong in a broad Nobel watchlist. The problem is not that the roster contains implausible people; it is that it gives very different reasons for inclusion similar prominence. The local roster contains 72 living, non-winning historical Clarivate Citation Laureates and 18 additional John Bates Clark Medal winners. A historical citation honor, a recent public prediction, and a promising early-career award are different signals.

There is no public official shortlist: nominations and deliberations remain confidential for 50 years. Use “editorial candidate pool,” not “Nobel nominees.” [Nobel nomination rules](https://www.nobelprize.org/nomination/)

Clarivate explicitly says its program does not predict recipients in a particular year. Its four 2026 economics selections are Susan Athey, Hal Varian, Michael Woodford and Sidney Winter. This is fresh recognition of Nobel-class work, not an official annual forecast. [Clarivate announcement](https://clarivate.com/news/clarivate-reveals-citation-laureates-2026-recognizing-transformative-scientific-breakthroughs/)

The roster covers all eight people discussed in NPR’s October 7 preview. It covers five of the ten principal names in Richard Tol’s September 20 model-based forecast. The omitted five are Barry Eichengreen, Drew Fudenberg, Tim Besley, David Weil and Gilles Saint-Paul. Tol’s forecast is one model, not a consensus ranking. [NPR preview](https://www.nprillinois.org/2026-10-07/who-will-win-the-2026-nobel-prize-in-economics), [Tol forecast](https://richardtol.substack.com/p/2026-nobel-memorial-prize)

## How to read the assessment

The assessments below are my editorial judgments, not estimated probabilities or claims about the committee:

- **Core:** a mature, distinct prize case that should be easy to find in the game. This includes both current public discussion and established contributions without fresh forecasting coverage.
- **Plausible:** a defensible member of the broad pool, but not someone I would promote as a current favorite solely on the evidence available.
- **Speculative:** a real scholarly contribution and a legitimate inclusion source, but a less straightforward prize case or weak recent Nobel-specific support.
- **Future:** a credible longer-horizon candidate; the inclusion evidence does not justify presenting them as a leading 2026 contender. This is not an age eligibility rule.

## Changes to the game I would prioritize

1. **Replace the current Featured sort.** It sorts by recognition year, so a 2026 Clark Medal can compete for top placement with decades of established work. Use an explicitly editorial, documented default, with alphabetical sorting available. Do not manufacture numerical winning odds.
2. **Separate the inclusion reason from current attention.** Badges could say “Clarivate 2026,” “Historical Clarivate,” “Named in a 2026 preview,” and “Clark Medal.” Keep dates and sources attached. Avoid a single opaque confidence score.
3. **Keep the whole broad pool searchable.** Put established contenders first and future/less-discussed names behind a filter, without preventing an informed player from backing an outsider.
4. **Show possible prize themes.** Empirical IO, New Keynesian monetary economics, credit cycles, trade, dynamic panels, behavioral economics, inequality and information economics are more useful than a flat list of famous people. Label these as possible combinations, not predicted official teams.
5. **Improve field labels.** Add IO/digital, public/health, political economy, and urban/spatial labels or allow multiple tags. In particular, change the misleading single labels for Levinsohn, Moore, Mauro, Glaeser and Pathak.
6. **Update the honors and familiar names.** Add the relevant Nemmers honors and Manski’s fresh BBVA recognition. Use familiar scholarly names while keeping stable candidate IDs unchanged.
7. **Do not let raw citation totals stand in for chances.** Methods papers, books and cross-disciplinary work generate very different citation patterns. The existing two-paper sparklines are not full-author annual trends; maintain that visible qualification.
8. **Use current affiliations or make the historical date visible on each card.** A general note is easy to miss. Currie is a concrete example of how a historical affiliation can mislead. [Current Yale faculty listing](https://catalog.yale.edu/gsas/degree-granting-departments-programs/economics/)

The first four changes would improve the pool’s usefulness more than adding another twenty famous names. Nothing in this review changes existing portfolios, candidate IDs, the roster or the application.
